
=================== SECTION IV PHYSICAL TESTBED ===================

IV. PHYSICALTESTBED ANDBURSTCHARACTERIZATION
A. Hardware Configuration
The transmitter contains an ESP32 microcontroller, a high-
intensity LED, and a transistor-based LED driver. The source
implementation identifies GPIO 23 as the LED control output
and uses on–off keying (OOK) with Manchester coding. The
receiver uses a BPW34 silicon PIN photodiode connected to
an LM358-based transimpedance amplifier (TIA), followed
by an ESP32 ADC input identified as GPIO 34. A software
OOK slicer and Manchester decoder recover the frame. An
SG90 servo-controlled flap is positioned between the optical
transmitter and receiver to create repeatable obstruction events.
The component choices are intended to make the testbed low-
cost and inspectable. The ESP32 provides the GPIO, ADC,
timing, and processing resources needed by the prototype.
The BPW34 provides a simple optical detector, while the
LM358 TIA converts the photodiode current into a measurable
voltage. The servo flap allows the duration and repetition of
physical blockage to be controlled more consistently than an
uncontrolled hand obstruction.
B. Optical Modulation and Sampling
The reported implementation uses a 1000 µs bit period,
corresponding to a raw rate of 1000 bps. Receiver sampling is
performed within a mid-bit window of approximately 450–550
µs into the bit interval. The mid-bit strategy is intended to
reduce sensitivity to transition transients and timing uncertainty.

The use of Manchester coding provides a transition structure

The physical system should not be interpreted as a com-

of-concept setting chosen for transparent physical recovery
experiments. The paper therefore evaluates recovery behavior
at the implemented rate rather than claiming performance at
the much higher rates reported by specialized LiFi systems.
C. Receiver Calibration
The reported receiver calibration measured dark ADC
values of approximately 0–5 counts and illuminated values
of approximately 95–176 counts. An operating threshold of
approximately 50 ADC counts was used. Manchester transition
consistency checks complemented CRC validation. These
measurements establish a substantial separation between the
observed dark and illuminated levels under the reported test
conditions, but the calibration is not assumed to remain constant
under arbitrary ambient illumination or geometry.
D. Burst Profiles
A burst is represented as a contiguous interval in which the
optical path becomes unavailable or unreliable. The logger
records start time, end time, duration, corrupted symbols,
affected bytes, frame identifier, and local signal information.
The study reports 56 empirical occlusion events across three
servo-driven profiles: P1 short blockage of approximately 15–
40 ms, P2 long blockage of approximately 150–380 ms, and
P3 mixed/randomized duration.
The empirical burst distribution is important for BASR.
Instead of masking independent characters with arbitrary
probabilities, the model is exposed to contiguous losses whose
duration is related to the physical obstruction process. The
source reports that physical obstructions corrupted approxi-
mately 10–25 contiguous bytes, which is substantially larger
than the four-symbol correction capability of the selected
RS(33,25) configuration in the evaluated cases.

E. Physical Failure Modes and Threat Model
The tested impairment is non-adversarial, physically gener-
ated burst loss. The system does not claim protection against
intentional frame forgery, telemetry spoofing, or an attacker
who injects syntactically valid false data. Three physical cases
remain particularly important: bursts longer than the training
distribution, repeated or overlapping obstructions outside the
measured profiles, and timing ambiguity near a CRC boundary.
These cases are relevant because the confidence model is
calibrated to the observed channel rather than to an arbitrary
worst-case distribution.

=================== SECTION V BASR ===================

V. BURST-AWARESEMANTICRECONSTRUCTION
A. Structured Payload Assumption
BASR is intended for telemetry in which fields have syntax
and contextual regularities. Examples used in the study include
•TEMP=27.4,HUM=61,MOTOR=ON,
•DEVICE=04,TEMP=26.8,STATUS=ACTIVE, and
•VOLT=3.72,CURR=0.42,STATE=OK.
The structured format is central to the method. A receiver
can exploit field names, delimiters, neighboring values, and
application constraints to generate a candidate reconstruction.
This does not imply that arbitrary encrypted binary payloads
can be reconstructed. The method is evaluated only on the
structured telemetry setting described by the source.

B. Edge-Constrained Transformer
BASR is implemented as an edge-constrained Transformer
encoder with model dimension dmodel = 64 , four attention
heads, two encoder layers, and feed-forward dimension dff =
96. The reported model contains 74,281 trainable parameters,
against a 75,000-parameter budget. This corresponds to 99.04%
utilization of the stated budget. The architecture is deliberately
compact; the goal is not to maximize language-modeling
capacity but to provide a small contextual predictor that can
be coupled to an embedded communication receiver.
For a candidate missing sequence ˆYm, exact reconstruction
accuracy is defined as the fraction of evaluated missing
segments that exactly match the transmitted segment. Task or
field accuracy is computed separately for application-relevant
fields. This distinction is important because a semantic receiver
can preserve a task-critical state while failing to reproduce
every character of the original message.
C. Semantic Failure Example
Consider a payload containing
TEMP=27.4,HUM=61,MOTOR=ON.
A reconstruction such as
TEMP=21.4,HUM=61,MOTOR=ON
may remain syntactically valid while being semantically wrong.
A CRC cannot certify such a reconstruction because the CRC
is calculated over the original transmitted bits. Consequently,
semantic plausibility and exact correctness must be separated
in the recovery decision.

The source defines the CGFP score as

The structural factor is deliberately binary. If the recon-

B. Burst-Severity Penalty
The physical burst information is incorporated through a
nonlinear nonincreasing penalty. The source records the penalty
parameters as α= 0.50 andγ= 1.15 . The purpose of the term
is qualitative and operational: as the fraction or span of missing
content increases, confidence in a contextual reconstruction
should not remain unchanged. The exact calibration is tied to
the studied payloads and measured burst distribution.
C. Selective Exact Fallback
When C < τ∗, the receiver issues a selective negative
acknowledgment (NACK) and requests the missing segment or
affected frame through the exact-recovery path. The recovered
content replaces the semantic candidate. This design means
that semantic inference is an optimization opportunity rather
than a mandatory reliability mechanism.
D. Illustrative Decision
For an illustrative frame with Ctoken = 0.93, Cphys = 0.90,
Penaltyburst = 0.95, and valid syntax, the resulting score is
C= 0.93×0.90×0.95≈0.795.(12)
Since this value is below τ∗ = 0.80 , CGFP rejects the
semantic candidate and invokes exact fallback. The example
demonstrates the conservative operating point: a candidate can
appear strong while still being rejected when its combined
confidence is marginal. If a candidate violates a hard structural
rule,S syntax = 0and fallback is immediate.

=================== SECTION XI ALGORITHMIC IMPLEMENTATION ===================

XI. ALGORITHMICIMPLEMENTATION ANDOPERATING
ENVELOPE
A. Receiver-Side Procedure
The receiver-side procedure can be expressed as a sequence
of deterministic communication operations followed by con-
ditional semantic inference. First, the ESP32 acquires the
optical waveform and applies the calibrated threshold. Second,
Manchester symbols are decoded and the frame delimiter,
length, sequence identifier, and CRC fields are parsed. Third,
a contiguous unavailable region is identified from missing
symbols, transition violations, or frame-level integrity failure.
Fourth, the receiver estimates the burst duration and affected-
symbol count and extracts the surviving context.
The semantic stage is entered only after the physical
processing has established a candidate loss interval. BASR
receives the surviving prefix, suffix, burst metadata, and payload
structure and returns a candidate missing segment with token-
level confidence. CGFP then evaluates structural validity and
computes the combined score. If the score is below the
threshold, the receiver does not deliver the semantic candidate.
Instead, it requests exact recovery. The final payload is then
subjected to the exact integrity mechanism available to the
receiver.

B. Pseudocode
The recovery logic can be summarized as follows:
1) initialize the optical receiver and calibrated threshold;
2) decode Manchester symbols and parse the frame;
3) if the frame is clean and CRC-valid, deliver it without
semantic inference;
4) otherwise, identify the unavailable interval and estimate TB
andN B;
5) extract surviving prefix/suffix context and generate a BASR
reconstruction;
6) computeC=C tokenCphysPenaltyburstSsyntax;
7) ifSsyntax = 0 orC <τ∗, issue NACK and recover exactly;
8) otherwise, deliver the semantic candidate and log the
decision;
9) record final correctness, field correctness, latency, and the
recovery path.
This procedure has an important operational property: a
clean frame bypasses the semantic model. Semantic inference
is used only when the physical layer has evidence of a loss
that cannot already be resolved by the normal integrity path.
This reduces unnecessary inference and prevents the model
from modifying error-free traffic.
C. Operating Envelope
The demonstrated operating envelope is defined by the
physical and computational parameters reported in the study:
1000 bps raw signaling, structured telemetry payloads, ESP32
transmitter and receiver nodes, BPW34 detection, LM358 TIA,
Manchester coding, and servo-driven blockage. Within this
envelope, the system has evidence from both simulation and a
small live experiment. Outside this envelope, the evidence is
incomplete.

D. Expected Behavior With Increasing Burst Severity
As burst severity increases, the amount of surviving context
decreases and the confidence penalty increases. The intended
behavior is therefore a gradual shift from semantic acceptance
toward exact fallback. At low severity, BASR has more context
and may produce a high-confidence candidate. At intermediate
severity, the confidence gate should accept only candidates
with strong structural and contextual evidence. At severe loss,
the system should increasingly reject semantic predictions and
use exact recovery.
This behavior is preferable to a fixed semantic-only policy
because the physical channel supplies information about the
reliability of the reconstruction. It also provides a direct experi-
mental hypothesis: acceptance rate should generally decrease as
normalized burst span increases, while false acceptance should
remain bounded if the calibration is valid. The current supplied
results do not provide a complete acceptance-versus-span curve,
so this relationship remains a design expectation rather than a
reported quantitative law.
E. Reviewer-Focused Evidence Matrix
A useful way to interpret the evidence is to map each major
claim to the experiment that supports it. The physical-link
claim is supported by clean-link measurements and the 56-event
occlusion campaign. The BASR capability claim is supported
by the reported exact and field-level accuracy values. The
CGFP safety claim is supported by accepted-patch precision,
patch-error rate, and the 15-frame live rejection behavior. The
retransmission-avoidance claim is supported only by simulation
because the live run accepted no patches. This separation
prevents the strongest simulation result from being inadvertently
presented as a hardware result.

=================== SECTION XII RESULTS ===================

XII. RESULTS
A. Clean-Link Physical Validation
The clean-link experiment reports a mean transit latency of
449.0 ms, with a range of 443–455 ms, zero bit errors, and
100% CRC-8 validation. This baseline is important because
semantic recovery should not be used to compensate for a
receiver that is already unreliable in the absence of occlusion.
B. Empirical Burst Behavior
The physical campaign contains 56 occlusion events. The
three profiles cover short, long, and mixed/randomized servo-
driven blockages. The source reports approximately 10–25
contiguous corrupted bytes during physical obstruction. This
observation is central to the motivation for burst-aware recovery:
the physical corruption interval can be larger than the correction
window of the selected RS-FEC configuration.
Because the supplied experimental record does not provide a
complete numerical table of mean, median, and maximum dura-
tion for every P1–P3 profile, those values are not reconstructed
here. Instead, the reported ranges and total event count are
retained. This is deliberate: filling missing profile statistics from
inference would compromise the reproducibility and accuracy
of the paper.
C. BASR Reconstruction Performance
The BASR model contains 74,281 trainable parameters.
The reported exact reconstruction accuracy is 42.63±1.47% .
Although exact character-level recovery is below half of the
evaluated missing segments, the reported motor actuation-state
field accuracy is 100.0%. This demonstrates the distinction
between exact source reproduction and task-relevant field
preservation.
The model’s mean edge CPU inference latency is reported as
2.79 ms. The parameter count corresponds to an approximate
290 KB footprint when stored as 32-bit floating-point weights
and approximately 73 KB under an idealized 8-bit representa-
tion. The latter is a storage estimate rather than a measurement
of the complete deployed binary or runtime memory footprint.

D. Confidence-Gating Operating Point
The reported CGFP threshold is τ∗ = 0.80. At this operating
point, approximately 27.15% of retransmissions are avoided
through accepted semantic patches in the evaluated simulation.
Accepted patches have 97.13% precision, and the reported
patch-error rate is 0.78%. These values are directly relevant
to the design objective because the main safety concern is not
merely rejection of a weak candidate but accidental acceptance
of an incorrect one.
The complete multi-point threshold sweep underlying the
selected threshold is not present in the supplied experimental
record. Therefore, the paper does not invent intermediate values
at thresholds such as 0.50, 0.60, 0.70, or 0.90. The threshold
should be regarded as a reported operating point requiring
release of the underlying sweep for full reproducibility.
E. End-to-End Comparison
The source reports the following end-to-end values. RS-FEC
recovers 37.5% of the real occlusion bursts and consequently
leaves a 62.5% UFER in that evaluated burst set. The 449.0 ms
value associated with the RS-FEC row is a clean-link reference,
not a claim that RS-FEC burst recovery itself completes in
449.0 ms. Stop-and-Wait ARQ reports 97.1% delivery at 1560.0
ms mean latency and 0.0% UFER.
Under continuous-burst simulation, SemLiFi reports 100.0%
delivery at 790.7 ms mean latency with UFER 1.00±0.13% .
Under the mixed condition in which 40% of frames experience
bursts, the reported latency is 592.5–595.5 ms with 100.0%
delivery. The source does not provide a comparable UFER
value for this mixed condition, so none is inserted.

G. Mixed-Burst Simulation
When 40% of frames experience bursts, SemLiFi reports
100% delivery and a latency range of 592.5–595.5 ms. This is
lower than the continuous-burst condition because fewer frames
require recovery actions. The result illustrates the expected
dependence of total recovery cost on burst prevalence, but it
should not be used to infer a continuous functional latency
curve because the source reports only this mixed operating
point.
H. Live Hardware Evaluation
The live hardware evaluation contains 15 frames. It reports
100% delivery, a latency range of 636.6–792.9 ms, and 0.00%
UFER. Importantly, all low-confidence semantic reconstructions
were rejected. The mean confidence was approximately 0.46,
below τ∗ = 0.80, and selective NACKs were therefore used
for all 15 frames.
This result is positive evidence for the safety behavior of the
gate under the tested severe conditions, but it is not evidence
that semantic patching is efficient on hardware. Since no
live frame was accepted as a semantic patch, the 27.15%
retransmission-avoidance result remains a simulation result. A
larger hardware campaign is required before claiming that the
simulated efficiency transfers to physical operation.
